Overview

Hozon New Energy Automobile Co., Ltd. was established on 16 October 2014 and operates the Neta passenger-car brand. Its 2024 application proof described a range spanning battery-electric and extended-range vehicles and positioned the brand at approximately USD15,000–45,000. In FY2023, vehicle and parts sales accounted for 97.8% of consolidated revenue. These are historical disclosures, rather than confirmation of an operating range at the research cutoff.[1][1][5][6]

The administrator disclosed in June 2025 that production had stopped in November 2024. The Jiaxing Intermediate People’s Court accepted the bankruptcy reorganization case on 12 June 2025, and transferred it to the Tongxiang People’s Court on 18 June. A January 2026 employee-claim notice confirms that the administrator was still investigating claims at that time. This review did not obtain the subsequent original restructuring plan, voting notice or court approval order; the latest plan terms, implementation and production-resumption status are therefore left unconfirmed.[5][6][1][5][6]

The verified financial series covers FY2021–2023. Revenue increased from RMB5,087 million to RMB13,555 million over that period, while attributable losses totalled approximately RMB18,314 million and gross margin remained negative in all three years. The financial table uses one consistent group reporting basis; later group results and the Thai subsidiary’s standalone results are not supplied without verified primary filings.[1][1][5][6]

Current operations

Business and products: historical operating base

The June 2024 prospectus describes the AYA series, including the Neta V, as small battery-electric SUVs; the X series, including the Neta U, as compact battery-electric SUVs; the L as a mid-size extended-range and electric SUV; the S as a sedan; and the GT as a two-door sports car. FY2023 model deliveries were 50,095 AYA, 46,724 X, 20,278 S and 7,092 GT vehicles, totalling 124,189. The overseas export figure was 17,019 units, or 13.7% of deliveries.[1][1][5]

The same document reports average vehicle selling prices of about RMB71,000 in 2021, RMB84,000 in 2022, RMB109,000 in 2023 and RMB113,000 in the first four months of 2024. Those figures describe the company’s historical product mix. They are not current retail quotations, and the prospectus’s USD positioning band is distinct from the administrator’s June 2025 description of an RMB80,000–200,000 product range.[1][5][1][5]

Technology and employees

The administrator lists the Shanhai and Yunhe vehicle platforms, Hao Zhi technology and Tiangong batteries among the company’s technologies. The prospectus records four R&D centres in Shanghai, Jiaxing, Beijing and Hong Kong. At 31 December 2023 the group had 7,932 full-time employees, including 2,132 in R&D, 3,096 in production, 2,249 in sales and marketing and 455 in general and administrative support. The R&D proportion was 26.9%; these are 2023 staffing figures, not a current headcount.[1][5][1][5]

Factories and production status

The June 2025 administrator announcement identifies the Tongxiang site and investments in the Yichun and Nanning production bases. It records Yichun as the second self-built plant, in production from 2022 with annual capacity of 100,000 vehicles, and Nanning as an 85.29%-held export knock-down base with capacity of 100,000. It also lists component factories in Tongcheng, Fengtai and Fengyang, Anhui. These are disclosed assets and nominal capacities, not verified current output.[5][1][5]

The administrator also identifies Thai and Indonesian plants operating under CKD arrangements, and says the Thai plant started production in 2024. Its announcement retrospectively dates the company’s stoppage to November 2024 and says more than 400 employees, including management and core technical personnel, remained when the announcement was issued. The statement is limited to its June 2025 disclosure date. No subsequent primary evidence of resumed series production was obtained.[5][1][5]

Domestic channels and overseas business

At end-2023 the Chinese sales network comprised 114 directly operated stores and 425 dealer stores, for a total of 539. The prospectus describes direct retail, city-partner dealers and bulk-purchase dealers serving customers such as ride-hailing operators, taxi companies and driving schools. Abroad it reported cooperation with about 80 dealers in 23 countries and regions at end-2023. These historical store and partner counts do not establish how many outlets still operate at the cutoff.[1][1][5]

The prospectus lists Neta Auto (Thailand) Co., Ltd. as a wholly owned group subsidiary, incorporated on 1 December 2021 for electric-vehicle and parts sales and after-sales services. Its registered capital was increased to THB232 million in April 2024. The original Thai standalone financial statements could not be checked, so this review provides no FY2022 or FY2023 revenue, profit or loss for that entity. Its current shareholding and operating status remain unverified.[1][1][5]

Founders and leadership

Founder and public career

Fang Yunzhou is identified as founder, executive director and chairman in the 2024 prospectus. Before establishing Hozon, he served as deputy general manager of Chery New Energy and headed the hybrid-power division of Chery’s central research institute. Hongli Zhihui’s September 2017 listed-company announcement identifies him as a Tsinghua University postdoctoral researcher and deputy director of Tsinghua’s energy-saving and new-energy vehicle centre. The Tsinghua experience is postdoctoral research, rather than doctoral study at that university. The prospectus records his engineering doctorate from Hefei University of Technology in June 2012 and his Tsinghua postdoctoral certificate in May 2017.[1][3][1][3][5][4]

Management and board: dated disclosures

The June 2024 prospectus names Fang as chairman, Zhang Yong as executive director and CEO, and Peng Qingfeng as executive director and vice president. It describes Fang’s remit as strategy, product and business development; Zhang’s as daily operations and implementation of business plans; and Peng’s as procurement, digital operations and supervision of component subsidiaries. These are the offices and responsibilities reported in that document, not a verified current management roster.[1][1][3][5][4]

The proposed post-listing board comprised three executive directors, five non-executive directors and four independent non-executive directors. The latter appointments were stated to take effect upon listing. This contingent arrangement does not establish who currently sits on the board. The administrator’s June 2025 announcement identifies Fang as legal representative and chairman. This review has not verified a later original appointment or departure notice, or a current CEO and board list.[1][5][1][3][5][4]

Equity, voting rights and state investors

The prospectus expressly defines Fang and his controlled entities as the controlling shareholder group. The capitalization table records their combined equity interest at 11.82%, while investors under concert-party arrangements held 20.28%. Fang could direct approximately 32.10% of voting rights through these arrangements. The group definition, economic shareholding and contractual voting rights are separate disclosures; this review does not substitute the mainland-law term “actual controller” for the prospectus’s defined term.[1][1][3][5][4]

The same pre-IPO capitalization table attributes 11.46% to Minsheng Equity, 9.82% to Huading Capital, 9.12% to 360 Security Technology, 5.35% to Cheng Zhizhong-controlled entities, 4.44% to Tongxiang Finance Bureau-related entities, 3.49% to Hefei Hi-tech and 3.04% to CATL. These are historical holdings, not a post-restructuring ownership table. The March 2022 Shenwan Hongyuan (Hong Kong) announcement separately identifies the relevant Yichun state investors’ government owner as Yichun, Jiangxi, China. Its older percentages are not mixed with the 2024 table.[1][4][1][3][5][4]

Competition

Competitive position and remaining assets

Our assessment: Neta’s historical competitive position should be evaluated through product coverage and delivery economics. The prospectus positioned the brand at approximately USD15,000–45,000 and itself described this as an intensely competitive segment. Its AYA and X ranges accounted for 96,819 of the 124,189 vehicles delivered in 2023, or approximately 78.0% by simple calculation. Although the company also offered S and GT models, the disclosed volume base remained concentrated in SUVs. This supports comparison with mainstream electric-vehicle offerings; a current ranking or named peer-volume comparison requires additional verified data.[1][1][5]

Our assessment: the factories, production qualifications and overseas channels provide a possible base for a restart, subject to continued availability. The administrator’s announcement documents the production qualifications and factory assets; the prospectus documents the historical export and dealer footprint. Their presence in dated disclosures is evidence of an operating base, but does not establish their current condition, continuing regulatory validity or commercial effectiveness. The value of those assets depends on what can actually be preserved and used.[1][5][1][5]

Economics and limits

Our assessment: rising average selling prices did not by themselves demonstrate a viable business model. The disclosed price increased from about RMB71,000 in 2021 to RMB109,000 in 2023, but gross margins were -34.4%, -22.5% and -14.9% across FY2021–2023, and operating cash flow remained negative. The narrowing gross loss is an improvement in that historical measure; it does not show that later production would generate cash. Product mix, procurement terms and utilization would all need to be reassessed using a verified restart plan.[1][1][5]

Our assessment: the overseas network offers an alternative commercial route, while adding a separate execution requirement. The historical export share of 13.7% and the Thai sales subsidiary establish that overseas business had begun before restructuring. They do not establish the subsidiary’s current profitability or the supply of parts and services today. A successful restart would need functioning local dealers and service arrangements as well as vehicles; if those relationships cannot be retained, the historical overseas footprint may yield less benefit than its nominal size suggests.[1][5][1][5]

Outlook

Public plans and the verification boundary

The 2024 prospectus stated an intention to deepen overseas business, develop Latin America, the Middle East and Africa, and subsequently consider Europe. The administrator’s June 2025 invitation sought potential restructuring investors to preserve asset value and support reorganization. These are dated plans and process steps. No verified original later investment agreement, restructuring plan or approval order was obtained in this review; investment amounts, future ownership, voting outcomes and production targets from secondary accounts are therefore not presented as established terms.[1][5][6][1][5][6]

Our analysis

Our assessment: the first test of a turnaround is a workable legal and operating framework. The verified record establishes entry into reorganization and a later employee-claim investigation; it does not establish an effective plan or completed financing. An assessment of a restart should therefore be conditional on verified approvals, usable funding and a defined operating scope. If those conditions are met, preserved assets could support production; if they are not, the available evidence is insufficient to forecast a production date or sales scale.[5][6][1][5][6]

Our assessment: the historical cash-flow record makes working capital as important as equipment. The group used approximately RMB2,991 million, RMB5,408 million and RMB4,354 million in operating cash in FY2021–2023. A restart that requires supplier advances, parts purchases and dealer support could consume cash before vehicle receipts arrive. This is a conditional inference from historical cash use, not a forecast of the required funding amount. Favorable procurement and payment terms could reduce that burden; slow sales or weak collections could increase it.[1][1][5][6]

Our assessment: service continuity should be examined by service and period. The prospectus describes dealers and agents providing maintenance and repairs, while the administrator later disclosed a production stoppage. That combination supports treating spare-parts supply, warranty handling and connected-car services as matters to verify during reorganization. It does not establish that every owner lost after-sales, warranty or OTA services. Continued operation by some service providers is also possible, and the actual interruption scope requires specific evidence.[1][5][1][5][6]

Our assessment: a recovery in deliveries would be one milestone, rather than proof of financial recovery. The historical series shows higher average selling prices alongside negative gross margin and operating cash outflows. A stronger recovery case would require verified evidence that vehicle sales cover production costs, that recurring expenses can be funded and that parts and service obligations can be maintained. Alternative outcomes include a smaller operating business or prolonged restructuring; the verified material does not support selecting one outcome as certain.[1][5][6][1][5][6]

Risks

Documented risk factors and proceedings

Financial exposure: the 2024 prospectus warned that historical gross losses, net losses, net current liabilities and negative operating cash flow could persist. It disclosed net current liabilities of approximately RMB1,991 million at end-2023. This is the company’s dated risk disclosure and financial position, not a verified current balance sheet.[1][1][5][6]

Supply and technology exposure: the prospectus warned that replacing highly customized components or qualifying alternative suppliers could take time and money, and that supply interruptions could interrupt production. It also said R&D spending might not produce the expected commercial benefits. These were disclosed contingencies, not findings that every supplier or technology arrangement had failed.[1][1][5][6]

Production and reorganization: the administrator’s June 2025 announcement disclosed the November 2024 production stoppage and the court’s acceptance of reorganization. The January 2026 notice stated that employee claims were still being investigated and that claims outside the first published batch remained under review. The notice is not a final total of employee liabilities, a repayment confirmation or an approval of a restructuring plan.[5][6][1][5][6]

Overseas execution: the prospectus described overseas business conducted through dealers, agents and local subsidiaries, with maintenance and repair roles allocated to those partners. The administrator later listed Thai and Indonesian CKD investments. The current status of those relationships and operations has not been verified. Service-continuity implications are discussed as our assessment in the outlook, rather than as a finding of universal service failure.[1][5][1][5][6]

现状

业务与车型：历史经营基础

2024 年 6 月招股书将 AYA 系列（含哪吒 V）列为小型纯电 SUV，X 系列（含哪吒 U）列为紧凑型纯电 SUV，L 为中型增程及纯电 SUV，S 为轿跑，GT 为双门跑车。2023 年各系列交付分别为 50,095、46,724、20,278、7,092 辆，合计 124,189 辆；同期海外出口为 17,019 辆，占交付量 13.7%。[1][1][5]

同一文件披露，平均售价由 2021 年约 7.1 万元升至 2022 年约 8.4 万元、2023 年约 10.9 万元，2024 年前四个月约为 11.3 万元。这组数据描述历史产品结构，并非当前零售报价。招股书的美元定位价格带，与管理人 2025 年 6 月所述 8 万–20 万元产品价格区间，也属于不同时间和用途的表述。[1][5][1][5]

技术与员工

管理人列举了山海、云河整车平台，以及浩智技术、天工电池等技术。招股书披露四个研发中心位于上海、嘉兴、北京和香港。截至 2023 年 12 月 31 日，集团全职员工共 7,932 人，其中研发 2,132 人、生产 3,096 人、销售及营销 2,249 人、一般及行政支持 455 人，研发占比为 26.9%。以上均为 2023 年人数，不作为当前员工规模。[1][5][1][5]

工厂与生产状态

管理人 2025 年 6 月公告列有桐乡厂区及宜春、南宁生产基地投资。其中宜春为第二座自建工厂，2022 年投产，年产能 10 万辆；南宁为持股 85.29% 的出口 KD 件基地，年产能 10 万辆。公告还列有安徽桐城、凤台、凤阳三个零配件工厂。这些是披露时的资产与名义产能，不代表当前实际产量。[5][1][5]

管理人同时列有泰国、印尼 CKD 工厂，并称泰国工厂于 2024 年投产。公告追溯披露公司自 2024 年 11 月起停产，发布时仍保留包括管理团队、核心技术人员在内的 400 余名员工。该人员留存及停产状态说明仅限于 2025 年 6 月的披露时点，本次未取得此后恢复批量生产的一手证据。[5][1][5]

国内渠道与海外业务

2023 年末，中国门店包括直营店 114 家、经销商门店 425 家，合计 539 家。招股书所述销售渠道包括直营、城市合伙人经销商，以及向网约车公司、出租车公司和驾校等批量供车的大客户经销商。海外方面，截至 2023 年末，公司与 23 个国家及地区约 80 家经销商合作。历史门店数和合作伙伴数不代表研究截止日仍在运营的网点数。[1][1][5]

招股书将哪吒汽车（泰国）有限公司列为集团全资子公司，成立于 2021 年 12 月 1 日，业务为电动汽车及零件销售和售后服务；其注册资本于 2024 年 4 月增至 2.32 亿泰铢。因未能核验泰国单体原始财报，本研究不列该公司 2022、2023 年收入、利润或亏损；其当前股权及经营状态也未核实。[1][1][5]

创始人与管理层

创始人与公开履历

2024 年招股书将方运舟列为创始人、执行董事、董事长。创立合众前，他曾任奇瑞新能源副总经理及奇瑞汽车中央研究院混合动力部负责人。鸿利智汇 2017 年 9 月上市公司公告列其为「清华大学博士后」、清华大学节能与新能源汽车中心副主任。因此，清华经历应写为博士后研究，不是于清华攻读博士。招股书另载，他于 2012 年 6 月取得合肥工业大学工学博士学位，2017 年 5 月取得清华大学博士后证书。[1][3][1][3][5][4]

管理层与董事会：分期披露

2024 年 6 月招股书列方运舟为董事长、张勇为执行董事兼 CEO、彭庆丰为执行董事兼副总裁。其所述职责分别是：方运舟负责战略、产品开发与业务发展；张勇负责日常运营和业务计划实施；彭庆丰负责采购、数字化业务及零部件子公司监督。以上为该文件时点的职务与分工，不作为当前管理层名单。[1][1][3][5][4]

文件拟定的上市后董事会包括三名执行董事、五名非执行董事及四名独立非执行董事，其中独立非执行董事任命以完成上市为生效条件。这一附条件安排不能用来确认现任董事会成员。管理人 2025 年 6 月公告仍列方运舟为法定代表人、董事长；本次未核验到此后的任免原件，也未取得现任 CEO 和完整董事会名单。[1][5][1][3][5][4]

股权、投票权与国资股东

招股书明确将方运舟及其控制实体定义为「控股股东集团」。资本化表中，其合计经济持股为 11.82%，一致行动安排项下相关投资者持股为 20.28%，方运舟通过相关安排可指示行使约 32.10% 投票权。集团定义、经济持股与协议投票权是分别披露的事项，本研究不将招股书定义的「控股股东集团」直接替换为中国内地法律语境下的「实际控制人」。[1][1][3][5][4]

同一上市前资本化表中，民生股权对应持股 11.46%、华鼎资本 9.82%、三六零安全 9.12%、程治中控制实体 5.35%、桐乡市财政局相关实体 4.44%、合肥高新 3.49%、宁德时代 3.04%。这些是历史股权，不是重整完成后的股权表。申万宏源香港 2022 年 3 月公告另明确，相关宜春国资投资主体对应的政府为中国江西省宜春市政府；该较早公告的持股比例不与 2024 年资本化表混用。[1][4][1][3][5][4]

竞争格局

竞争位置与可延续资产

我们的判断：哪吒历史竞争位置应结合产品覆盖和交付经济性考察。招股书将品牌定位于约 1.5 万–4.5 万美元区间，并自述这是竞争激烈的细分市场。2023 年 AYA 与 X 系列合计交付 96,819 辆，占总交付 124,189 辆的约 78.0%（简单计算）。虽已有 S、GT 等车型，披露的销量基础仍集中于 SUV。这支持将其放在主流电动车产品中比较；当前排名或指定同行的销量对比，还需另有可核验数据。[1][1][5]

我们的判断：工厂、生产资质与海外渠道可以构成复产基础，前提是这些资产和资源能够继续使用。管理人公告列明生产资质及工厂资产，招股书记录历史出口及经销商网络；有日期的披露能说明此前存在经营基础，但不能确认当前设备状态、资质持续有效性或渠道商业效果。这些资产的意义取决于重整中实际保留了什么，以及后续能否重新投入经营。[1][5][1][5]

经济性与限制

我们的判断：平均售价上升本身尚不足以证明商业模式成立。2021–2023 年披露的平均售价由约 7.1 万元升至 10.9 万元，但三个财年的毛利率分别为 -34.4%、-22.5%、-14.9%，经营现金流均为负。毛损收窄代表历史指标改善，不能直接证明此后复产能够产生现金。产品结构、采购条件和产能利用情况，都需要结合可核验的复产方案重新评估。[1][1][5]

我们的判断：海外渠道提供了另一条商业路径，同时增加本地执行要求。历史出口占比 13.7% 及泰国销售子公司的存在，说明海外业务在重整前已开展，但不能据此确认子公司当前盈利能力，或今天仍能供应备件、提供服务。复产除整车外，还需要当地经销商及服务安排实际运作；若相关合作无法保留，历史海外布局能够发挥的作用可能小于名义规模。[1][5][1][5]

未来展望

公开计划与核验边界

2024 年招股书提出深化海外业务，发展拉美、中东和非洲市场，并随后适时考虑欧洲。管理人 2025 年 6 月预招募公告则以推进重整、维护资产价值为目的招募潜在投资人。这些均为有明确日期的计划及程序步骤。本次未取得可核验的后续投资协议、重整计划或批准裁定原件，故不将二手报道中的投资额、未来股权、表决结果和产销目标列为已确认条款。[1][5][6][1][5][6]

我们的分析

我们的判断：扭转经营首先需要可执行的法律与经营框架。已核验材料能确认进入重整及此后的职工债权调查，尚不能确认计划已生效或融资已完成。因此，讨论复产应以可核验的批准文件、可使用资金及明确经营范围为前提。条件满足时，保留资产可能支持生产；条件未满足时，现有证据不足以预测复产日期或销量规模。[5][6][1][5][6]

我们的判断：历史现金流说明，营运资金与设备同样关键。集团 2021–2023 年经营活动现金净流出分别约 2,991、5,408、4,354 百万元。若复产需要预付供应商款、采购零部件并支持经销商，整车回款前可能已有现金支出。这是依据历史现金消耗作出的条件分析，不是对所需资金的金额预测。采购与付款条件改善可以减轻压力，销售缓慢或回款不足则可能扩大压力。[1][1][5][6]

我们的判断：服务持续性应按服务种类与期间逐项核验。招股书描述了经销商及代理商提供维修保养的安排，管理人此后披露停产；两者支持在重整中关注备件、质保处理及车机服务，但不能据此概括所有车主的售后、质保或 OTA 均已中断。部分服务商继续运营也是可能情形，具体中断范围仍需对应证据。[1][5][1][5][6]

我们的判断：交付恢复可以是一个进展节点，尚不能单独证明财务恢复。历史数据体现平均售价上升，但毛利和经营现金流仍为负。更有力的恢复依据应包括整车销售能够覆盖生产成本、经常性支出有资金来源，以及备件和服务义务能够持续履行。其他可能包括缩小经营规模或重整延续；现有已核验材料不足以将某一种结果确定化。[1][5][6][1][5][6]

风险

已披露风险因素与程序事项

财务事项：2024 年招股书提示，历史毛损、净亏损、流动负债净额及经营现金净流出可能持续，并披露 2023 年末流动负债净额约 1,991 百万元。这是公司在相应日期的风险披露及财务状况，不作为当前资产负债表。[1][1][5][6]

供应与技术事项：招股书提示，高度定制零部件的替代开发、供应商重新认证可能需要时间和成本，供应中断可能影响生产；同时提示研发投入未必取得预期商业效果。这些是文件披露的或有风险，不代表已确认所有供应商或技术合作均已失效。[1][1][5][6]

生产与重整事项：管理人 2025 年 6 月公告披露了 2024 年 11 月停产，以及法院受理重整。2026 年 1 月公示明确职工债权尚在调查，未列入首批名单的债权继续审核。该公示不是职工债务最终总额、还款完成证明，也不是法院批准重整计划的文件。[5][6][1][5][6]

海外执行事项：招股书所述海外业务通过经销商、代理商及当地子公司开展，维修保养职责分配给相应合作伙伴；管理人随后列有泰国、印尼 CKD 投资。本次未核实这些合作关系及经营的当前状态。服务持续性的影响放在展望中作为判断讨论，不将其写成所有服务全面失效的既定事实。[1][5][1][5][6]
