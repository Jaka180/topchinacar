BYD's 32.2% Share of China's NEV Exports Slipped in July — Because the Market Grew Faster Than It DidBYD's 32.2% Share of China's NEV Exports Slipped in July — Because the Market Grew Faster Than It Did

CPCA data for July 2026 shows BYD exporting 173,721 passenger NEVs for a 32.2% share, down from 34.2% in June even as its volume rose 121.7% year-on-year. Tesla China's 66,330 units lifted the Shanghai plant into third place in China's NEV export ranking, behind Chery's 82,768. With total China NEV exports at 540,000 units and domestic NEV retail down year-on-year for seven straight months, the export line has become the industry's growth engine — and the ranking's arithmetic reveals two very different playbooks underneath the headline numbers.

By TopChinaCar Editorial作者：TopChinaCar 编辑部 · Published August 15, 2026发布于 2026-08-15 · Compiled from the primary sources cited below基于文末所列一手信源编写

BYD exported 173,721 passenger new energy vehicles in July 2026, a 32.2% share of China's NEV export market, up 121.7% year-on-year and 1.7% month-on-month, according to the China Passenger Car Association's monthly ranking. Its share was 34.2% in June. Volume up, share down — the market grew faster than the leader did.

Tesla China exported 66,330 vehicles, up 83.4% from June and 143.2% year-on-year, moving the Shanghai operation into third place in the NEV export ranking with a 12.3% share. Chery held second with 82,768 NEV exports, a 15.3% share, up 195.4% year-on-year and 12.1% month-on-month.

The denominator explains the compression. China's total NEV exports reached 540,000 units in July, up 147.8% year-on-year, with NEVs accounting for 58.8% of all passenger-vehicle exports. Domestic NEV retail sales, meanwhile, have now declined year-on-year for seven consecutive months. The volume that is not being absorbed at home is leaving the country, and it is leaving from more than a handful of loading docks.

Include combustion models and the hierarchy inverts. Chery led overall passenger-vehicle exports in July with a 21.7% share, ahead of BYD's 173,721 units (18.9%) and Geely's 106,220 (11.6%). SAIC Passenger Vehicle was fourth at 73,563 units, Tesla China fifth with 66,330 (7.2%), Changan and GWM sixth and seventh, and Leapmotor tenth with a 1.9% share.

That gap between the two tables is the most useful number in the release. Chery's group-wide exports hit a record 202,533 vehicles in July, its fifth consecutive monthly record — against 82,768 NEV exports. The majority of what Chery ships is still combustion. BYD's overall passenger-vehicle export figure, by contrast, is identical to its NEV figure: 173,721 in both rankings. One company is running a mixed-powertrain export book; the other is running a single-technology one and betting the whole overseas franchise on it.

Anyone who has sat on the supplier side of an OEM's overseas program knows which of those is harder to hold together. A mixed book lets you fill a vessel and a distributor's forecourt with whatever the destination market's fuel prices, charging infrastructure and duty schedule reward this quarter. A pure-NEV book gives you scale economics and a cleaner parts list, but no fallback if a single market's demand assumptions move. Both approaches produced records in July. They will not both survive the same shock.

The cumulative figures show the ordering is not a one-month artifact. For January-July, Chery led overall passenger-vehicle exports with 1,130,649 units (21.9% share, up 70.6% year-on-year), BYD was second at 943,051 (18.2%), and Geely third at 578,676 (11.2%). Tesla China ranked sixth with 5.7%, Leapmotor tenth at 113,863 units (2.2%).

BYD's own disclosure gives the sharpest read on where its center of gravity now sits. Overseas deliveries of passenger vehicles and pickups reached a record 179,841 units in July — roughly 43% of total monthly sales. A company selling more than four in ten vehicles outside its home market is no longer an exporter with an international division. It is a multinational that has to duplicate, in each destination, the operating functions it built once in China.

That is where these rankings stop being informative. Export volume is measured at the port; it says nothing about whether an OEM can operate in a market three years later — whether it has a supplier ecosystem that can support local assembly, whether its labor practices survive a local audit, whether it has the government relationships to weather a policy change. Those variables move slowly and are far harder to stand up than a production line. The monthly ranking will keep rewarding whoever loaded the most containers. The durable winners will be decided by things this table cannot see.

Tesla's presence at number three cuts across the usual framing. The Shanghai Gigafactory has long served both domestic demand and export markets including Europe, and its climb up the NEV ranking reflects that continued role as a global Tesla supply node rather than the arrival of a new manufacturing entrant. It also means that trade and tariff measures aimed at Chinese-made vehicles now catch a US automaker's China-built output in the same net as BYD's and Chery's — 66,330 units of it in July alone. The "China versus global OEM" export narrative is harder to sustain when the third-largest NEV exporter out of China is Tesla.
