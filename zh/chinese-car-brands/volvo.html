Overview

Volvo Cars (Volvo Car AB) is a Swedish premium car maker founded in Gothenburg in 1927 and majority-owned by China's Zhejiang Geely Holding Group, which acquired the company from Ford in 2010. The operating and listed entity, Volvo Car AB, has been listed on Nasdaq Stockholm under the ticker VOLCAR B since its initial public offering on 29 October 2021, which raised about SEK 20 billion; the largest shareholder, Geely Sweden Holdings AB, held 78.65% of shares and capital as of the FY2025 report.[1][2][18]

Volvo Cars sells premium SUVs, sedans and estates in more than 100 countries, built around safety and Scandinavian design. Its line-up spans mild-hybrid and plug-in-hybrid versions of the XC60, XC40, XC90 and XC70 SUVs, and the fully electric EX90, EX60, EX30, ES90 and EM90.[15] In 2025 the company sold 710,042 cars (down 7%), of which 46% were electrified, and posted revenue of SEK 357.3 billion; group operating income (EBIT) fell to SEK 0.3 billion and the group posted a net loss of SEK 3.0 billion (net income attributable to the parent of SEK 174 million).[14][2][3]

Within Geely Holding's portfolio, Volvo Cars is the group's premium brand of Swedish origin and sits alongside Geely Auto, Zeekr, Lynk & Co, Polestar and Lotus; it is a separately listed company that reports its own financials under IFRS.[2] Entering 2026, the company faces a sharp China decline and US pressure, and has intensified its turnaround: an additional SEK 5 billion in cost cuts, about 2,500 global job reductions, and the withdrawal of its full-year volume guidance.[7][8]

History

  1. 1927

    Volvo Cars is founded in Gothenburg, Sweden by Assar Gabrielsson (President) and Gustaf Larson (director of engineering); the first car, the ÖV4, drives out of the factory on 14 April 1927. The Volvo trademark had been registered by SKF in 1915.[16][30]

  2. 1999

    AB Volvo sells its car division to Ford Motor Company for about US$6.45 billion, separating Volvo Cars from the Volvo trucks, buses and construction-equipment group.[21]

  3. 2010-03

    Zhejiang Geely Holding Group and Ford sign a stock purchase agreement in Gothenburg on 28 March 2010 for Geely to acquire 100% of Volvo Car Corporation for USD 1.8 billion, including a USD 200 million note.[21][28]

  4. 2010-08

    The acquisition is completed on 2 August 2010 at a ceremony in London: Geely issues the note and pays USD 1.3 billion in cash, and names Stefan Jacoby as Volvo Cars' new President and CEO (to take up the role on 16 August 2010), with Li Shufu as Chairman.[28]

  5. 2013

    Volvo Cars opens its first complete manufacturing plant outside Europe in Chengdu, China, initially producing the S60L and XC60.[16]

  6. 2015

    The second-generation XC90 debuts, the first model on the Scalable Product Architecture (SPA) platform, launching a new design language and technology generation.[16]

  7. 2017

    Volvo announces its electrification strategy (from 2019 all new models to be electrified), becoming one of the first legacy premium carmakers to do so; the compact XC40, its first CMA-platform model, is also launched.[24][16]

  8. 2018

    Volvo Cars inaugurates its first US plant in Charleston (Ridgeville), South Carolina, making it a manufacturer with plants in Europe, Asia and North America.[16]

  9. 2019

    Volvo unveils its first fully electric SUV, based on the XC40 — launched as the XC40 Recharge and later renamed the EX40 — its first model with Android-based infotainment.[16]

  10. 2021-10

    Volvo Car AB lists on Nasdaq Stockholm on 29 October 2021 under the ticker VOLCAR B, pricing its IPO at SEK 53 per share and raising about SEK 20 billion; Geely Sweden Holdings remains the largest shareholder.[18]

  11. 2022

    Jim Rowan, formerly of Dyson and BlackBerry, succeeds Håkan Samuelsson as CEO and President, tasked with accelerating the software-led and electrified transformation.[10]

  12. 2024-09

    Volvo Cars adjusts its 2030 ambition, shifting from a fully-electric-only goal to 90-100% electrified (BEV plus PHEV) by 2030, allowing limited mild hybrids.[24]

  13. 2025-01

    Volvo Cars signs a share purchase agreement with Northvolt AB for Northvolt's shares in NOVO Energy AB, leading to Volvo Cars taking full (100%) ownership of the battery joint venture, subject to approvals.[27]

  14. 2025-04

    The board reappoints Håkan Samuelsson as CEO and President from 1 April 2025 for a two-year term, succeeding Jim Rowan; Volvo also launches a SEK 18 billion cost-and-cash action plan and names Fredrik Hansson CFO.[10][11][1]

  15. 2025-11

    At its Strategy Update 2025, Volvo Cars sets a long-term target of an EBIT margin above 8% and positions the SPA3-based EX60 mid-size SUV as its key electrified-growth enabler.[13]

  16. 2026-09

    The board appoints Klaus Zellmer, CEO of Škoda Auto, as President and CEO, to assume the role no later than 1 October 2027; Håkan Samuelsson stays on to ensure a smooth transition.[9]

  17. 2026-10

    Volvo Cars posts a Q3 2026 operating loss and, on 2 October, withdraws its full-year 2026 volume and cash-flow guidance; it announces an additional SEK 5 billion in cost cuts and about 2,500 job reductions.[7][8]

Current operations

Business structure

Volvo Cars is a single-brand premium car maker whose revenue is dominated by vehicle sales, supplemented by parts and accessories, used cars, contract manufacturing and (declining) emissions-credit sales.[25] It employed about 42,600 full-time staff in 2025, with head office, product development, marketing and administration in Gothenburg, and R&D/design centres in Gothenburg and Shanghai.[15] Production plants are located in Gothenburg/Torslanda (Sweden), Ghent (Belgium), Ridgeville/Charleston (South Carolina, US), and Chengdu, Daqing and Taizhou (China).[15]

Technology and platforms

Volvo's range is built on scalable vehicle architectures: the Scalable Product Architecture (SPA) introduced with the second-generation XC90 in 2015, the smaller Compact Modular Architecture (CMA) introduced with the XC40 in 2017, and the new SPA3 architecture that underpins the EX60 electric SUV.[16][13] The company positions the SPA3-based EX60 as the key enabler of its electrified growth and cites the shared hardware and software platforms with Geely Holding as a cost-structure advantage.[13]

Regional footprint

Europe is the largest market (332,667 cars in 2025, down 10%), followed by China (149,549, down 4%), the US (121,607, down 3%) and other markets (106,219, down 5%); China, the US, the UK, Germany and Sweden were the five largest markets in 2025.[14] In 2025 the electrified share reached 46% (24% fully electric in Q4), which Volvo describes as the highest among legacy premium peers; the company reduced CO2 emissions per car by 31% versus its 2018 baseline.[26]

Latest results (as of 2026-10-29)

In H1 2026 retail sales were 324,800 cars (down 8%). Q2 2026 revenue was SEK 77.7 billion with operating income of SEK 0.8 billion (EBIT margin 1.1%) and net income of SEK 0.4 billion; the company said it delivered its SEK 5 billion full-year cost-saving target six months early.[5][25]

Q3 2026 was tougher: revenue of SEK 66.9 billion (down 22.6%), an operating loss of SEK 5.6 billion (EBIT margin -8.4%) and free cash flow of SEK -8.9 billion, driven by China revenue down nearly 50% year on year, US tariff effects, a SEK 1.1 billion CAFE-credit revaluation and recognition of Polestar losses.[7] Q3 retail sales were 141,609 cars (down 10.7%), with Europe and other markets up 2%, the Americas down 14% and Greater China down 40.6%.[6]

Cost and structural actions

After the 2025 SEK 18 billion cost-and-cash plan, the company announced in Q3 2026 an additional SEK 5 billion of indirect-cost and variable-cost reductions, cutting about 2,500 jobs globally (2,000 in Sweden) and targeting a nearly 25% reduction in total indirect spend by end-2027 versus FY2024.[7] It plans to concentrate production in five dedicated Volvo plants globally and is exploring multi-brand co-assembly with Geely at the Chengdu and Ghent plants.[7]

Battery joint venture

NOVO Energy, the Gothenburg battery joint venture with Northvolt, became fully owned by Volvo Cars after a January 2025 share purchase agreement; Volvo paused its operations in January 2026 while it searches for a new technology partner.[27][35]

Founders and leadership

Founders

The company was founded in 1927 in Gothenburg by Assar Gabrielsson and Gustaf Larson, initially as a subsidiary of the ball-bearing maker SKF; Gabrielsson became President and Larson director of engineering. Their guiding principle was safety.[30][16]

Chairperson and key executives (as of 2026-10-29)

  • Eric Li (Li Shufu) — Chairperson of the Board, re-elected at the 2026 AGM; he is the chairman and main owner of Geely Holding and has chaired Volvo Cars since the 2010 acquisition.[12][18]
  • Håkan Samuelsson — President and CEO since 1 April 2025, on a two-year term; previously Volvo Cars CEO from 2012 to 2022 and later Polestar chair until 2024. Earlier he was CEO of MAN and an executive at Scania.[10]
  • Klaus Zellmer — appointed President and CEO in September 2026, to assume the role no later than 1 October 2027; currently Chairman of the Board of Management and CEO of Škoda Auto, formerly a board member at Volkswagen Passenger Cars (sales/marketing) and a more than 20-year Porsche executive.[9]
  • Fredrik Hansson — Chief Financial Officer since 24 April 2025, succeeding Johan Ekdahl; previously Volvo's Head of Global Controlling & Performance Steering, Deputy CFO and a former McKinsey partner.[11]
  • Erik Severinson — Chief Commercial Officer, who comments on the company's global sales performance.[6]

In China, the company appointed Duan Jianjun as President and CEO of Volvo Cars Greater China in May 2026, succeeding Yuan Xiaolin, and He Kuo as President of the Greater China sales company in September 2026.[22][23] Jim Rowan, CEO from 2022, stepped down on 31 March 2025.[10]

Board and ownership

After the AGM of 31 March 2026, the board comprises Eric Li (chair), Håkan Samuelsson, Daniel Li (Li Donghui), Diarmuid O'Connell, Jonas Samuelson, Lila Tretikov, Ruby Lu (Rong Lu), Pieter Nota, Natalie Knight and Markus Schäfer (the latter effective 1 July 2026). The AGM resolved that no dividend be paid for FY2025 and re-elected Deloitte AB as auditor.[12]

Geely Sweden Holdings AB holds 78.65% of shares and capital, itself owned by Shanghai Geely Zhaoyuan International Investment Co., Ltd., ultimately owned by Zhejiang Geely Holding Group.[2]

Competition

Competitive scope (our assessment)

Our assessment: Volvo Cars competes in the global premium segment against BMW, Mercedes-Benz and Audi, in fully electric models increasingly against Tesla, and in China against domestic premium EV makers. This positioning is based on its product portfolio and pricing, which in China ranges from about RMB 160,000 (EX30) to RMB 818,000 (EM90).[36] In Europe it is one of the strongest legacy brands in fully electric sales, with a 45% BEV share in Q3 2026.[7]

Strengths and weaknesses (our assessment)

Our assessment: the brand's main assets are its safety reputation, Swedish heritage and the resources of Geely Holding, which provide shared hardware sourcing, platforms and capital, letting Volvo cut costs and develop regional products; management explicitly cites the Geely relationship as a cost-structure advantage.[13] In Europe its BEV position is strong and the EX60 had more than 50,000 orders by Q3 2026.[7]

Our assessment: but profitability is thin — FY2025 group EBIT was only SEK 0.3 billion on revenue of SEK 357.3 billion and the group swung to a net loss, so the company relies on cost cutting rather than pricing power.[2] In China its sales fell 40.6% in Q3 2026 amid what the company calls hypercompetition from local brands, and in the US tariffs and the removal of EV incentives continue to pressure margins.[6][7]

Our assessment: compared with the largest global premium and mass-market brands, Volvo's roughly 710,000-unit scale (2025) is mid-tier, limiting its ability to amortise R&D and platform costs; its high exposure to a shrinking premium segment in China is a structural pressure.[14][7]

Outlook

Company plans

At its Strategy Update 2025, Volvo Cars set a long-term target of an EBIT margin above 8% with strong positive cash flows and growth through electrification, with the SPA3-based EX60 as the key enabler.[13] It plans its largest-ever product push of 13 new cars between now and end-2030, including six China-specific models developed with Geely; in 2027 it will reveal three, one for Europe and two China-specific.[7] In 2027 it will also introduce its next global BEV, aimed at a larger, lower-priced segment in Europe with first deliveries after the summer, and it is exploring multi-brand assembly with Geely at its Chengdu and Ghent plants.[7] It also plans to localise XC60 production in Charleston in Q1 2027 and to concentrate on five dedicated plants globally.[7] For 2026 it aimed to return to full-year volume growth and better free cash flow, but withdrew this guidance on 2 October 2026 citing a weaker market.[8] These are the company's stated targets, not achieved results.

Our analysis

Our assessment: near-term performance hinges on the EX60 ramp in Europe (which the company says is slower than planned due to cell-to-body technology) and the XC70 and long-range PHEVs, offsetting the China decline and US headwinds; the company itself expects a significantly stronger second half of 2026 in sales than the first, supported by Europe and a US recovery.[5] A return to a mid-to-high single-digit EBIT margin depends on cost reduction, the common hardware/software platform with Geely and regionalised production taking hold; the withdrawal of 2026 volume guidance and the Q3 2026 loss show the plan is not yet secure.[7] The succession to Klaus Zellmer (by October 2027) introduces execution risk during a critical turnaround, but also signals a planned, orderly handover.[9]

Risks

  • China market decline and hypercompetition: Greater China deliveries fell 40.6% in Q3 2026 and China revenue fell nearly 50% year on year, as local premium EV brands exert severe price pressure.[6][7]
  • US tariff and regulatory pressure: US import tariffs, the removal of EV incentives and a SEK 1.1 billion CAFE-credit revaluation from US legislation changes weighed on margins in 2025-2026.[7]
  • Thin profitability: FY2025 group EBIT was SEK 0.3 billion and the group posted a net loss of SEK 3.0 billion; Q3 2026 was a loss-making quarter, so resilience depends on continued cost actions.[2][7]
  • Impairment and electrification-transition risk: the SEK 11.4 billion EX90/ES90 platform impairment in Q2 2025 shows that EV-platform lifecycle profitability can deteriorate and trigger charges.[3]
  • Battery-joint-venture uncertainty: after Northvolt's collapse, Volvo took full ownership of NOVO Energy but paused its operations in January 2026 pending a new technology partner, leaving battery sourcing for the Gothenburg gigafactory unresolved.[27][35]
  • Raw-material and pricing pressure: rising raw-material costs and an industry-wide price war, combined with a shrinking premium market, pressure margins and volumes.[5]
  • Related-party and financing exposure: Volvo Cars is 78.65% controlled by Geely and its financial results incorporate its stake in loss-making Polestar; Q3 2026 included recognising Polestar's losses.[2][7]
  • Management succession: the CEO handover to Klaus Zellmer by October 2027 carries execution risk during a critical turnaround.[9]

概览

沃尔沃汽车（Volvo Car AB）是 1927 年创立于瑞典哥德堡的豪华汽车制造商，现由中国浙江吉利控股集团控股——吉利于 2010 年从福特手中收购该公司。其运营与上市主体 Volvo Car AB 自 2021 年 10 月 29 日首次公开募股（募资约 200 亿瑞典克朗）起在纳斯达克斯德哥尔摩以代码 VOLCAR B 挂牌；截至 2025 财年报告，第一大股东吉利瑞典控股（Geely Sweden Holdings AB）持有 78.65% 股份与资本。[1][2][18]

沃尔沃汽车在全球 100 多个国家销售以安全与北欧设计著称的豪华 SUV、轿车与旅行车，产品线涵盖 XC60、XC40、XC90、XC70 等轻混/插混 SUV，以及 EX90、EX60、EX30、ES90、EM90 等纯电车型。[15] 2025 年公司销量 710,042 辆（同比 -7%），其中 46% 为电气化车型；营收 3,573 亿瑞典克朗，集团营业利润（EBIT）降至 3 亿瑞典克朗，集团净利润为 -30 亿瑞典克朗（归母净利润 1.74 亿瑞典克朗）。[14][2][3]

在吉利控股的多品牌矩阵中，沃尔沃汽车是集团旗下扎根瑞典的豪华品牌，与吉利汽车、极氪、领克、极星、路特斯并列；它是独立上市公司，按 IFRS 单独披露财务。[2] 进入 2026 年，公司面临中国市场大幅下滑与美国市场压力，遂强化扭亏计划：追加 50 亿瑞典克朗降本、全球裁员约 2,500 人，并撤回全年销量指引。[7][8]

历史沿革

  1. 1927

    沃尔沃汽车在瑞典哥德堡创立，创始人为阿瑟·加布里埃尔松（总裁）与古斯塔夫·拉尔森（工程总监）；1927 年 4 月 14 日首款车 ÖV4 下线。「Volvo」商标 1915 年由 SKF 注册。[16][30]

  2. 1999

    沃尔沃集团（AB Volvo）以约 64.5 亿美元将轿车业务出售给福特汽车，沃尔沃汽车自此与沃尔沃卡车、客车及工程机械集团分离。[21]

  3. 2010-03

    浙江吉利控股集团与福特于 2010 年 3 月 28 日在哥德堡签署股权收购协议，以 18 亿美元收购沃尔沃轿车公司 100% 股权，其中 2 亿美元以票据方式支付。[21][28]

  4. 2010-08

    收购于 2010 年 8 月 2 日在伦敦完成交割：吉利开出票据并支付 13 亿美元现金，并任命斯特凡·雅各比为沃尔沃汽车新一任总裁兼 CEO（8 月 16 日履新），李书福任董事长。[28]

  5. 2013

    沃尔沃汽车在成都建成欧洲以外首家完整整车工厂，最初生产 S60L 与 XC60。[16]

  6. 2015

    第二代 XC90 亮相，成为首款基于 SPA（可扩展产品架构）平台的车型，开启全新设计语言与技术代际。[16]

  7. 2017

    沃尔沃宣布电气化战略（自 2019 年起所有新车型均电动化），成为最早作出该承诺的传统豪华车企之一；同年推出首款 CMA 平台车型——紧凑型 SUV XC40。[24][16]

  8. 2018

    沃尔沃汽车在美国南卡罗来纳州查尔斯顿（里奇维尔）建成首家美国工厂，成为横跨欧、亚、北美三大洲的整车制造商。[16]

  9. 2019

    沃尔沃推出首款纯电 SUV（基于 XC40）——上市时名为 XC40 Recharge、后更名 EX40，也是首款搭载安卓车机系统的车型。[16]

  10. 2021-10

    沃尔沃汽车于 2021 年 10 月 29 日在纳斯达克斯德哥尔摩上市，代码 VOLCAR B，发行价每股 53 瑞典克朗、募资约 200 亿瑞典克朗；吉利瑞典控股仍为第一大股东。[18]

  11. 2022

    曾任戴森与黑莓高管的骆文襟（Jim Rowan）接替汉肯·萨穆埃尔松出任 CEO 兼总裁，负责加速软件主导与电动化转型。[10]

  12. 2024-09

    沃尔沃汽车调整 2030 年目标，由「纯电专属」调整为 2030 年电气化车型（纯电+插混）占比 90%–100%，允许保留少量轻混车型。[24]

  13. 2025-01

    沃尔沃汽车与 Northvolt AB 就后者持有的 NOVO Energy AB 股份签署股权收购协议，沃尔沃汽车将取得该电池合资公司 100% 股权（尚待相关批准）。[27]

  14. 2025-04

    董事会任命汉肯·萨穆埃尔松自 2025 年 4 月 1 日起再度出任 CEO 兼总裁，任期两年，接替骆文襟；公司同时推出 180 亿瑞典克朗降本增效计划，并任命 Fredrik Hansson 为 CFO。[10][11][1]

  15. 2025-11

    在 2025 年战略更新发布会上，沃尔沃汽车设定长期息税前利润率超 8% 的目标，并将基于 SPA3 平台的中型纯电 SUV EX60 定位为电气化增长的核心。[13]

  16. 2026-09

    董事会任命斯柯达汽车 CEO 克劳斯·泽尔默（Klaus Zellmer）为总裁兼 CEO，最晚于 2027 年 10 月 1 日履新；汉肯·萨穆埃尔松留任以确保平稳交接。[9]

  17. 2026-10

    沃尔沃汽车公布 2026 年三季度经营亏损，并于 10 月 2 日撤回全年销量与现金流指引；宣布追加 50 亿瑞典克朗降本并全球裁员约 2,500 人。[7][8]

现状

业务结构

沃尔沃汽车是单一品牌的豪华车企，收入以整车销售为主，辅以零部件与配件、二手车、代工制造及（持续下滑的）碳积分销售。[25] 2025 年公司约有 42,600 名全职员工，总部、产品开发、市场与行政职能位于哥德堡，并在哥德堡与上海设有研发/设计中心。[15] 生产工厂位于瑞典哥德堡/托斯兰达、比利时根特、美国南卡罗来纳州里奇维尔（查尔斯顿），以及中国的成都、大庆与台州。[15]

技术与平台

沃尔沃的产品线基于可扩展整车架构：2015 年随第二代 XC90 推出的 SPA（可扩展产品架构）、2017 年随 XC40 推出的 CMA（紧凑模块化架构），以及支撑 EX60 纯电 SUV 的全新 SPA3 架构。[16][13] 公司把基于 SPA3 的 EX60 定位为电气化增长的核心抓手，并将与吉利控股共享硬件与软件平台视为成本结构优势。[13]

区域布局

欧洲是最大市场（2025 年 332,667 辆，同比 -10%），其后是中国（149,549 辆，-4%）、美国（121,607 辆，-3%）与其他市场（106,219 辆，-5%）；中国、美国、英国、德国、瑞典是 2025 年五大市场。[14] 2025 年电气化车型占比达 46%（四季度纯电占比 24%），公司称其在传统豪华车企中电气化占比最高；单车二氧化碳排放较 2018 年基线下降 31%。[26]

最新业绩（截至 2026-10-29）

2026 年上半年零售销量 324,800 辆（同比 -8%）。二季度营收 777 亿瑞典克朗、营业利润 8 亿瑞典克朗（EBIT 利润率 1.1%）、净利润 4 亿瑞典克朗；公司称全年 50 亿瑞典克朗降本目标已提前六个月完成。[5][25]

三季度形势更为严峻：营收 669 亿瑞典克朗（同比 -22.6%）、营业亏损 56 亿瑞典克朗（EBIT 利润率 -8.4%）、自由现金流 -89 亿瑞典克朗，主因是中国区营收同比下滑近 50%、美国关税影响、11 亿瑞典克朗 CAFE 积分重估损失以及确认极星亏损。[7] 三季度零售 141,609 辆（同比 -10.7%），其中欧洲及其他市场 +2%、美洲 -14%、大中华区 -40.6%。[6]

降本与结构调整

继 2025 年 180 亿瑞典克朗降本增效计划后，公司于 2026 年三季度宣布追加 50 亿瑞典克朗的间接支出与可变成本削减，将全球裁员约 2,500 人（其中瑞典 2,000 人），目标到 2027 年底整体间接支出较 2024 财年下降近 25%。[7] 公司计划将生产集中于全球五个专用沃尔沃工厂，并正在成都与根特工厂探索与吉利的「多品牌共线」组装。[7]

电池合资公司

与 Northvolt 在哥德堡合资的电池公司 NOVO Energy 在 2025 年 1 月签署股权收购协议后由沃尔沃汽车全资持有；沃尔沃于 2026 年 1 月在其寻找新合作伙伴期间暂停了该公司运营。[27][35]

吉利瑞典控股持有 78.65% 股份与资本，其上层为上海吉利兆圆国际投资有限公司，最终由浙江吉利控股集团持有。[2]

竞争格局

竞争范围（我们的判断）

我们的判断：沃尔沃汽车处于全球豪华车细分市场，与宝马、奔驰、奥迪竞争，纯电车型与特斯拉形成竞争，在中国市场还面临本土高端电动车品牌的竞争。这一判断依据其产品矩阵与定价——在华价格从约 16 万元（EX30）到 81.8 万元（EM90）不等。[36] 在欧洲，它是纯电销量表现最强的传统豪华品牌之一，2026 年三季度纯电占比达 45%。[7]

优势与短板（我们的判断）

我们的判断：品牌核心资产是安全口碑、北欧传承与吉利控股的资源支持——后者提供共享硬件采购、平台与资金，使沃尔沃能够降本并开发区域化产品；管理层明确将吉利关系视为成本结构优势。[13] 在欧洲其纯电地位稳固，EX60 截至 2026 年三季度订单超 5 万辆。[7]

我们的判断：但盈利薄弱——2025 财年集团 EBIT 仅 3 亿瑞典克朗（营收 3,573 亿瑞典克朗），集团由盈转亏，公司更多依赖降本而非定价能力。[2] 在中国，三季度销量同比下滑 40.6%，公司称面临本土品牌的「极度竞争」；在美国，关税与电动车补贴退出持续挤压利润。[6][7]

我们的判断：与最大的全球豪华及大众品牌相比，沃尔沃约 71 万辆（2025 年）的规模处于中游，摊薄研发与平台成本的能力有限；其对持续收缩的中国传统豪华细分市场的高敞口，构成结构性压力。[14][7]

未来展望

公司规划

在 2025 年战略更新发布会上，沃尔沃汽车设定长期息税前利润率超 8%、保持强劲正现金流并以电气化驱动增长的目标，核心抓手是基于 SPA3 平台的 EX60。[13] 公司计划推进史上最大规模产品攻势：从现在到 2030 年底推出 13 款新车，其中含 6 款与吉利合作开发的中国专属车型；2027 年将发布 3 款（1 款欧洲车型、2 款中国专属）。[7] 2027 年公司还将推出下一款全球纯电车型，主攻欧洲更大、价格更低的主流细分市场，首批交付自夏季后开始；并正探索与吉利在成都、根特工厂进行多品牌共线生产。[7] 公司还计划 2027 年一季度在查尔斯顿本地化生产 XC60，并将全球生产集中于五个专用工厂。[7] 就 2026 年，公司原拟恢复全年销量增长并改善现金流，但已于 2026 年 10 月 2 日以市场疲软为由撤回该指引。[8] 以上为公司目标表述，并非已实现结果。

我们的分析

我们的判断：近期表现取决于 EX60 在欧洲的爬坡（公司披露因 cell-to-body 新工艺而慢于原计划）以及 XC70 等长续航插混车型放量，以对冲中国下滑与美国压力；公司自身也预期 2026 年下半年销量将显著强于上半年，依托欧洲与美国复苏。[5] 能否回到中高个位数 EBIT 利润率，取决于降本、与吉利的通用硬件/软件平台以及区域化生产是否落地；2026 年销量指引的撤回与三季度亏损显示该计划尚未稳固。[7] 最晚 2027 年 10 月的 CEO 交接（由克劳斯·泽尔默接任）在关键扭亏期引入执行风险，但也意味着一次有计划的平稳过渡。[9]

风险

  • 中国市场下滑与极度竞争：大中华区三季度交付同比下滑 40.6%、当季中国区营收同比下滑近 50%，本土高端电动车品牌带来严峻价格压力。[6][7]
  • 美国关税与监管压力：美国进口关税、电动车补贴退出，以及因美国法规调整而计提的 11 亿瑞典克朗 CAFE 积分重估损失，在 2025—2026 年持续挤压利润。[7]
  • 盈利薄弱：2025 财年集团 EBIT 仅 3 亿瑞典克朗、集团净亏损 30 亿瑞典克朗；2026 年三季度陷入亏损，抗风险能力依赖持续降本。[2][7]
  • 减值与电气化转型风险：2025 年二季度对 EX90/ES90 平台计提 114 亿瑞典克朗减值，表明纯电平台生命周期盈利可能恶化并引发计提。[3]
  • 电池合资公司不确定性：Northvolt 破产后，沃尔沃取得 NOVO Energy 全部股权，但因尚未确定新合作伙伴，于 2026 年 1 月暂停其运营，哥德堡电池工厂的电池供应来源仍未落实。[27][35]
  • 原材料与价格战压力：原材料成本上行、行业价格战叠加豪华市场收缩，压制销量与利润率。[5]
  • 关联方与融资敞口：沃尔沃汽车由吉利持股 78.65% 控制，财务并表受其持股的亏损公司极星影响；2026 年三季度确认了极星亏损。[2][7]
  • 管理层交接：最晚 2027 年 10 月 CEO 由克劳斯·泽尔默接任，在扭亏关键期存在执行风险。[9]
